# User Interview Notes — [Project Name]

Use this template to take notes in interviews with target users before you start building an MVP. Keep each field short. The goal is not to get approval for your idea but to learn the real problem and the context in which people would use a solution.

## Context and profile

1. Could you briefly introduce yourself and your work?
2. In your daily work, which tasks do you deal with related to [the problem area]?
3. What takes the most time in this area? What does it cost you (time, money, stress)?

## Current solution and pain point

4. How do you solve this problem today? (Tools, manual work, other products — "I don't solve it" is also an answer.)
5. What is the most frustrating part of your current solution?
6. Have you looked for a product for this before? Why didn't you buy it, or why did you stop using it?

## Ideal solution and expectations

7. If you could solve this problem with a magic wand, what would the result look like?
8. If this product had to do one job perfectly for you, what would that job be?
9. Which features are nice to have, and which are must-haves? Why?

## Decision process and payment

10. Who makes the decision for a product like this? You, or does someone else need to approve it?
11. What would you consider a reasonable price for a similar solution? (A price expectation or range.)
12. What would make this product worth buying for you?

## Risks and doubts

13. Why might you not trust this product?
14. What would be the biggest obstacle stopping you from trying it?

## Outcome and next step

- Most important thing learned: [One sentence]
- Assumption that turned out wrong: [If any]
- Next step: [Follow-up date / email / second interview]

## Note-taking tips

- Don't try to write a word-for-word transcript. Capture the essence of each answer and pay attention to the words the person uses.
- Let the user describe the problem in their own words; don't ask them to describe your product.
- Instead of collecting "great idea!" answers, focus on whether they would pay, and why.
- At the end of the interview, agree on the next step: a calendar invite, an early-access list, or a second interview.

## Adapting it

- **Internal tools** (e.g. a payroll dashboard for one organization): replace questions 10–12 with "Who must approve this?", "What would make you stop using the current method?" and "What would make you trust its numbers?"
- **Course or student feedback:** drop the payment section; ask what helped, what was confusing, and what they would change, and keep responses anonymous where possible.
- **Evidence:** interview notes are user-reported claims. Treat them as evidence about needs and opinions, not as verified facts (see `RESEARCH_PROTOCOL.md`).
